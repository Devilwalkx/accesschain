# Usability session plan (results: NOT RUN)

No sessions with older or disabled people have been run. Nothing in this repository is a usability result, and the author's own walk-through does not count as one. This is the plan for when sessions are possible.

## Who
At least 5 people per group, recruited by someone other than the developers, not told how the app works: (a) people aged 70 or over who use a smartphone for messages or calls; (b) people who use a wheelchair or a walking aid or avoid steps; (c) people who use a screen reader or a very large text size. Record the phone model, Android version, text size and whether TalkBack is on. Compensate participants and get consent for any recording; do not record faces unless needed.

## Tasks (one at a time, read aloud, no help)
1. "You want to visit Tate Modern from London Bridge. You cannot manage steps. Find out whether you can get there." (first run: they must tell the app their needs first)
2. "Say how far you can walk, then look again." (change a need)
3. "The app says it is not sure about part of the way. What would you do next?" (do they understand the four parts and 'What you can do')
4. "Start from somewhere other than a station." (postcode; also try 'Where I am now' and say no to the phone's question)
5. "Make the writing bigger." (Home text size or Settings)
6. "Turn off the internet and try again." (offline messages)
7. "Delete everything the app has about you."

## No-coaching protocol
Read each task exactly as written. Do not point, explain, or confirm. If stuck for 2 minutes, say "What would you do if I were not here?"; after a further minute, mark the task failed and show the next one. Do not answer questions about the app's meaning; note them.

## What to record
Task success without help (yes/no), time to finish, every wrong tap, every moment of hesitation, what they said the result meant in their own words (did they understand "not confirmed" is not "no"?), whether they noticed the demonstration label, whether the text and buttons were large enough, any physical difficulty (tremor, small targets), and what they would do next. Also: did anyone act on 'What you can do', and did anyone read a status as a promise.

## Success criteria to agree before the sessions
Decide the pass mark in advance (for example: at least 4 of 5 per group complete tasks 1 and 3 unaided and can say in their own words what "not confirmed" means). Results go in a new file; the status stays NOT RUN until that file exists.
